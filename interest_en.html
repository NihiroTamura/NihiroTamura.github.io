Research InterestsRESEARCH INTERESTS

I focus on two aspects: human-like movement in androids and natural, human-like interaction between people and androids.

Human-likeness of android motion

My work so far has concerned human-like motion in robots, and androids in particular: robots whose appearance closely resembles that of a human being. The closer the appearance, the more an observer expects the movement to be equally natural, and the less any deviation escapes notice.

Traditionally this has been split into two separate problems: generating a motion trajectory, and realizing that trajectory on the hardware. Trajectory generation, however, tends to rely on subjective evaluation and lacks quantitative metrics, while trajectory realization has limited reproducibility because it depends on the physical characteristics and control performance of the android. Treating the two apart makes human-like motion hard to reach.

I therefore proposed an approach that treats the human-likeness of realized motion quantitatively, assuming the target trajectory can be reproduced stably. For control I adopted Active Disturbance Rejection Control (ADRC), which suppresses disturbances and yields stable responses even for complex nonlinear systems. Engineering metrics were computed from motion data obtained by varying the ADRC parameters, and subjective perceptions of human-likeness were quantified through pairwise comparison experiments. As a result, we found that the ranking trends of the engineering metrics generally agreed with those of the subjective evaluations, and proposed a method for designing human-like motion based on control parameters.

Users and Customers

Once androids become part of everyday life, two kinds of people will stand around them: the user, who instructs the android, and the customer, who receives a service from it. In a restaurant, the user is the owner, the customer is the diner, and the android is the waiter.

Most existing work on androids has focused on how naturally they can interact with customers. n contrast, comparatively little attention has been paid to interactions with users— the people who instruct and operate the androids. However, a user may also become a customer when they leave the environment in which they operate the android.

If users cannot interact with androids as naturally as they would with another person when giving instructions, it may be difficult for them to accept androids as human-like entities. Therefore, I aim to develop a system that allows users to interact with androids naturally, just as they would when giving instructions to another person.

Current work

I am working to develop a system that allows users to interact with androids naturally.

To achieve this, it is essential for an android to reliably reproduce the movements instructed by the user. However, because androids have body structures similar to those of humans, the effects of gravity can change significantly depending on their posture, making it difficult to achieve stable motion across different positions.

So far, I have improved the stability and responsiveness of android motion by using Active Disturbance Rejection Control (ADRC). However, stable motion remains challenging in positions where the effects of gravity change significantly.

To address this issue, I am now introducing feedforward control in addition to ADRC. Feedforward control anticipates the forces required for the android to move and provides them in advance. By combining these two control approaches, I aim to enable androids to move stably across a wide range of postures.
